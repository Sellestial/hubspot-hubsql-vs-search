# Article review 2

Codex (gpt-6-sol) reviews the revised draft.

The updated figures’ numbers match the `facts.md` update. The remaining problems are mostly claims that reach beyond those numbers.

1. **The 10× claim needs a boundary.** Quote: “With code, search does too, 10x slower” in the [cover] and “With code, search catches up, but it’s about 10 times slower” in the article. A skeptical engineer will point out that this holds for the five questions selected for the [time figure], not all ten. The other gaps were smaller, and search was faster once. Codex’s gaps were also smaller. **Replace the headline with:** “On five questions where both coded Claude setups were right, search took 8–13 times longer.” Say in the article that these are five examples, and label the 524 calls and 191 seconds as medians of three runs.

2. **The no-code takeaway promises too much.** Quote: “Without code, HubSQL is the difference between an answer and an estimate” and “HubSQL gets it right.” Claude got 28/30 with HubSQL, not 30/30; search got 19/30, so it could answer some questions. **Replace the takeaway with:** “Without code, Claude scored 28/30 with HubSQL and 19/30 with search alone. On the contact-industry top 10, HubSQL got all three runs right; search got none right.” Make the conclusion similarly specific.

3. **The matching-rule heading and causes figure overstate the mechanism.** Quote: “Text filters matched words, not text, in both tools”; the [causes figure] says “Where the 38 wrong answers came from” and marks all 20 RevOps misses red. The evidence shows broader matches than the scoring definition, but does not establish a general “matches words” rule: 8,920 matches differs even from the 8,916 titles containing both words anywhere. Five of the 20 RevOps misses also gave other wrong numbers. **Replace the article heading with:** “These MCP filters returned more records than a literal match.” **Replace the figure title with:** “Wrong answers by question, across 360 runs.” Its red note should say that 15 of the 20 RevOps misses reported 10,347.

4. **The pilot `LIMIT` claim is presented as a verified HubSQL fault.** Quote: “HubSQL’s grouped counts can come back low with a small LIMIT” and “below the real number.” That observation came from the earlier, unscored test with custom tool wrappers; the supplied facts compare larger limits with search totals, not an export-verified “real number.” An engineer could reasonably ask whether the wrapper or pilot setup caused it. **Replace with:** “In an unscored pilot using custom tool wrappers, a job-title query with `LIMIT 10` returned lower, variable counts. I did not establish that behavior in the 360 scored runs.” Removing the bullet would also keep the article focused.

5. **Make the controlled comparison unmistakable.** Quote: “Two models, with and without code.” The following text explains it, but the lead can sound as if both models ran both ways. The methods also omit the matched HubSQL-with-code scores from the prose. **Replace with:** “Claude Sonnet 5.5 supplied the with-code and without-code comparison. Codex was a separate coding-agent check.” Later: “With code, Claude scored 29/30 with search and 28/30 with HubSQL; Codex scored 30/30 and 28/30.”

6. **The cover’s run count implies that all runs are plotted.** Quote: “360 runs on our portal” beneath bars showing 180 runs. The [accuracy figure] fixes this, but the cover has not. **Replace with:** “Plain setups shown: 180 of 360 runs on our portal, October 2026.”

7. **One agent-behavior claim is not supported by the supplied evidence.** Quote: “In the transcripts, the agents that looked at the matched records caught this. The ones that trusted the first count didn’t.” The aggregate files establish the extra California record, but do not substantiate that categorical account of agent behavior. **Replace with:** “The extra ‘Estado de Baja California’ record was visible in the returned rows; the total of 13,564 alone did not reveal it.” Keep the original only if the published transcripts document every case.

8. **Disclose the tool path, and simplify the date example.** Quote: “HubSpot’s own MCP server, the connector AI assistants use” leaves out the common custom proxy described in the experiment file. **Replace with:** “Every setup reached HubSpot’s remote MCP server through our proxy, using the server’s unchanged read-only tool definitions; HubSQL setups also had `query_crm_data`.” For “The same date filter covered different days,” use: “On our portal, a September filter returned 86 contacts as an MCP HubSQL count but 90 when grouped by source; the grouped result included October 1. REST HubSQL returned 86 either way.”

9. **The audit link is still a publication blocker.** Quote: “The test harness, the questions, the selection rules and the results are on GitHub: [REPO LINK].” The figures also direct readers to that repo. **Replace the placeholder with a working link and check that it contains the promised results**, or narrow those claims to what is actually public.

**Verdict: not ready yet.** Fix the cover and timing scope, the matching and pilot claims, and the repository link before publishing. The first review’s main experimental objections are otherwise substantially addressed: the code effect is tested within Claude, the time figure says both setups were right, the tool labels and selection disclosure are clearer, and the accuracy figure states its 180-run scope. The tone is direct, with no prohibited wording or em dashes.
